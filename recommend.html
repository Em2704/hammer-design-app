Notes on the job spec

  1. * Task — what the work is. Only hammering has study data today; more tasks come later.
  2. * Material struck — the surface the hammer hits. Knob (scaffold fitting) and Wood were measured directly. Rubber and Plastic are ranked from the Knob data, Metal and Concrete from the Wood data, matched by hardness; those results are flagged as approximate.
  3. * Hammer weight — head weight of the hammer you use. The study measured a 15 oz Stiletto (S) and 16, 20 & 22 oz Estwings; any other weight is interpolated between the two nearest studied hammers.
  4. * Hammer brand — a label for your own reference. It is not used in the score.
  5. * Task Duration — how long the repetitive, continuous hammering lasts. When no strike count is given it is converted to strikes at 30 per minute; 30 minutes (≈ 900 strikes) is the reference load that fatigue and workload are scaled against.
  6. * Strikes made — a rough count is fine. When filled in it replaces the duration estimate for the load calculation; leave it blank if you don't know.

Strain report

What you get. A Your hammer card for the exact weight you entered, then the four hammers the study measured — 15 oz Stiletto and 16, 20 & 22 oz Estwing — ranked from least to most overall strain for this job.

How to read it. Every bar runs 0–100 and lower means less load on the body. Four components make up the score: muscle effort, impact force, muscular fatigue and overall workload. Hover the Each metric has one of these. Hover or focus it to see how the number is calculated and what it is measured from. beside any metric to see how it is calculated.

Where it comes from. Group means from the CISWP field study of scaffolders and carpenters, striking scaffold knobs and wood. Differences between hammers on the knob surface were statistically significant; on wood they were not, so treat the wood ordering as descriptive rather than a firm ranking.
